Studying usually means juggling a dozen tabs, scattered notes, and half-remembered links.

You find something useful in a research paper, another insight in a YouTube lecture, a good quote in a [[articles/12657586-how-to-add-annotations-to-documents-on-desktop|PDF]] - and somehow, it all ends up lost or forgotten. Buried in Google Drive, or worse, nowhere at all. Sounds familiar?

That's where **Cluing helps.**

## The Student Loop

A simple workflow that takes you from scattered sources to a finished essay — without losing anything along the way.

**_Save → Understand → Test yourself → Find gaps → Write_**

-   **Save** highlights from any source as you read, with your own notes attached

-   **Understand** by asking AI Chat to explain, summarize, or break down what you've saved

-   **Test yourself** by asking AI to generate a quiz based on your own material

-   **Find gaps** by asking what's missing or underrepresented in your research

-   **Write** directly in Canvas, with your Topics as context

Each step builds on the last. And because everything stays connected to its original source, nothing gets lost between them.

## Save What Matters, When It Matters

With **[[articles/11839482-how-to-install-the-cluing-extension-and-create-your-first-topic|Cluing]]**, you can save key ideas from any source - academic articles, **[[articles/12267724-cluing-reader|PDFs]]**, YouTube videos, forums - with just one click.

Each highlight (_we call it a **snippet**_) stays linked to the **original source** and comes with your own notes, comments, tags, or reminders.

![](/media/2544593311/Visual-1---EXT.webp)

You can save key insights **from any source**:

-   Textbooks (PDF or web)

-   Lecture videos (YouTube)

-   Online articles and blogs

-   Discussion forums like Reddit, X, or LinkedIn

Every snippet you save includes:

-   **What you found** - the exact sentence or quote you saved

-   **Why it matters** - your comment, question, or thought right next to it

-   **Where it came from** - every snippet is linked to the original source

## A structure that actually fits the way you study

Cluing lets you organize everything into layers of structure:

-   **Snippets** - The highlights you save from any source

-   **Hashtags** \- Tag snippets by subject or theme (e.g. #cellstructure, #frenchrevolution)

-   **Topics** - Group snippets that belong to the same concept (e.g. “Photosynthesis,” “World War II sources”)

-   **Clusters** - Bigger buckets for your study goals (e.g. “Biology Final Prep,” “History Essay Research,” “Group Project – Climate Change”)

So instead of bouncing between folders and files, you’ve got everything in one place - **connected**, **searchable**, and easy to review.

## Upload Your Course Materials

Got lecture slides, assigned readings, or research papers as PDFs? **[[articles/15442354-upload-documents-to-ai-chat|Upload them directly into AI Chat]]** and start asking questions, without reading the whole thing first.

-   _"What is the central argument of this paper?"_

-   _"Summarize the methodology and findings"_

-   _"What are the limitations the authors acknowledge?"_

Save the most relevant AI-extracted points back as Snippets into your Topic - sitting right next to everything else you've collected, properly sourced.

## Study with AI Chat That Knows What You've Been Reading

Once your Topic has enough snippets, open **[[articles/12606209-ai-chat-in-cluing|AI Chat]]** and **[[articles/15444365-mention-a-topic-in-ai-chat|mention it]]**.

This is where context does the real work. AI Chat doesn't give you generic textbook summaries. It works with **your notes, your highlights, and the comments you've added** as you saved. The richer your snippets, the sharper the answers.

Use it to actually study, not just search:

-   **Test yourself:**

    -   _"Generate a 10-question quiz based on everything in @BiologyFinalPrep"_

    -   _"Ask me five difficult questions about the material in @HistoryEssay and tell me if my answers are correct"_

-   **Find gaps:**

    -   _"Based on what I've saved in @MicroeconomicsNotes, what topics am I missing or haven't covered enough?"_

    -   _"Are there any contradictions between the sources in @TheoryOfFirm?"_

-   **Make connections:**

    -   _"What do you see connecting @TheoryOfFirm and @MarketStructures?"_

    -   _"What are the main counterarguments to the thesis I'm developing in @HistoryEssay?"_

The **quiz prompts** are especially useful before an exam. Instead of rereading everything, you let AI test you on your own material and surface exactly where the gaps are.

## Write Essays and Papers in Canvas

When it's time to write, open **[[articles/12701026-ai-canvas|Canvas]]** from AI Chat. With your Topics in context, ask AI to help structure your thinking:

-   _"Draft an outline for a 2000-word essay on market failures, using my @MicroeconomicsNotes"_

-   _"Write an introduction based on the argument I've built in @HistoryEssay"_

-   _"Suggest a structure for my literature review based on the papers I've saved"_

Canvas opens with a draft ready to edit - rewrite in your own voice, restructure sections, add citations - without switching between apps.

## Learning is social - so is Cluing

Forget sending emails overloaded with links and "check this when you can" messages.

-   Share your work with anyone using a **[[articles/11792146-sharing-topics-with-clu-ing-links|Clu.ing link]]**. Classmates or tutors can reply directly in context, without juggling tabs.

-   **[[articles/11793611-how-to-mention-collaborators-in-comments|@mention collaborators]]** in a comment and they'll get notified via email or [[articles/11776028-installing-the-cluing-slack-app|Slack]]

-   [[articles/11792004-permission-levels-explained|You stay in full control]] of what you share - View, Comment, or Edit access for each person.

## Ready whenever you need it

When it's time to write an essay, prepare a study guide, or work on a group project, **[[articles/15644219-export-options|export your research]]** in whatever format fits:

1.  **[[articles/11749010-how-to-make-a-copy-of-a-topic|Export entire Topics]]** - including all comments - as an exact duplicate, even into a different workspace.

2.  **[[articles/11775995-copy-all-your-snippets-with-comments|Copy all snippets]]** and paste straight into Google Docs, Word, Notion, or any editor.

3.  **[[articles/11768139-download-topic-as-a-csv-file|Download as CSV]]** to open in Google Sheets or Excel.

4.  **[[articles/11748989-print-or-save-a-topic-as-pdf|Save as PDF or print]]** - perfect for offline review or sharing.

5.  **[[articles/11775739-drag-drop-snippets-to-external-sources|Drag and drop]]** snippets directly into Gmail, Notion, or Google Docs without extra formatting.
