The Description is where you brief the Task on this specific job. Same Agent, same ground rules - the deliverable, the inputs, the constraints, and what to do with it when it's done.

## What to put in every Description

Four things carry the weight. Everything else is optional.

-   **_The deliverable_** - what you want back, in specific terms

    -   A 900-word blog draft

    -   A summary I can paste into Slack

    -   A shareable link

-   **_The inputs_** - where the Agent should pull from

    -   My Topic 'Team Workflows'

    -   The attached CSV

    -   The last three posts I published

-   **_The constraints_** - tone, length, format, deadline, anything that would change the shape of the output.

    -   Match the tone of our published posts

    -   Under 500 words

    -   By Monday 9 AM

-   **_Where it lands_** - what should happen with the result.

    -   Save it to my workspace

    -   Give me a shareable preview link

    -   Post it in #marketing

You don't need all four every time - a research question might not have a "where it lands." But if you skip all of them, the Agent guesses. And guesses are how you end up with something close.

## A weak Description vs a strong one

### Weak:

-   _Write a blog post about how small teams work_

The Agent doesn't know how long, what tone, who reads it, or where it goes.

### Strong:

-   _Write a 900-word blog post about how small teams manage research. Pull ideas from my saved Topic 'Team Workflows', match the tone of my last three published posts, add a hero image, and save the draft to my workspace._

Same idea. But the Agent now has a target, a source, a voice, and a destination.

## Write it like a brief, not a prompt

The Description isn't a chat message. It's the kind of note you'd leave for a new teammate who's picking up the work while you step out.

You wouldn't say "can you write me a blog post?" to a teammate and walk away. You'd tell them who it's for, how long, what tone, and where to put it when it's done. The Agent works the same way.
